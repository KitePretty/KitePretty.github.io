## Overview

Questions about breast health can arise after noticing a bodily change, arranging a consultation, undergoing tests, or preparing for treatment. This project examines how people bring those questions to an online community and what kinds of peer support they receive. I initiated the project to understand these experiences and develop research directions for supporting communication and care.

## Approach

I analyzed a corpus of 15,037 public Reddit threads. Latent Dirichlet allocation (LDA) identified ten discussion contexts, from discovering lumps and waiting for results to reading clinical reports. I constructed a stratified purposeful sample of 1,000 threads for content analysis of posts and replies.

The study examines expressed needs across four positions before treatment: appraisal, help-seeking, diagnostic investigation, and treatment preparation. Its two-stage framework distinguishes access to a human response from the presence of theoretically corresponding support categories in replies.

The current extension, *Routing, Not Reading*, explores how technology, potentially including AI, might help people articulate support needs and prepare questions for clinical conversations. Digital ethnography and outreach to clinicians and patients inform this developing co-design direction.

## Findings

Earlier posts more often ask what a symptom or finding means. Questions about procedures and next steps, along with requests for emotional relief, become more prominent closer to diagnosis and treatment.

Receiving a reply does not necessarily mean receiving the kind of support a post calls for. Examining both response access and reply content makes these differences visible. In an exploratory analysis of 100 threads about reading clinical reports, 32 received a human reply and 13 contained informational support. These measures describe replies rather than whether a person’s needs were met or their health improved.
