My research interests lie in human–computer interaction and social computing. I study how technology can support people before diagnosis, during healthcare encounters, and through recovery. I am interested in how people navigate uncertainty, communicate their needs, and organize support, both inside healthcare institutions and in everyday settings beyond the healthcare system.

My research experience spans patient portals, online health communities, and eating-disorder mutual-aid organizations. Across these settings, I examine how people make sense of health information and give and receive support, including the work required to care for those who help others.

I apply mixed methods to understand these experiences. I enjoy moving from identifying a problem to finding the communities it matters to and working with them to co-design technologies or systems. I see AI as potentially complementing rather than displacing human connection. I remain skeptical that technology can solve every problem and situate my research in the particular contexts and relationships through which care takes place.
